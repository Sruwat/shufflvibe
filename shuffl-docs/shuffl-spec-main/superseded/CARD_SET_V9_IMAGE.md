# Card Set v9 — a room, not a claim

A card is no longer a statement about what you value. It is **a detail of a room you either
want to be in or you do not**.

## Why

A sentence naming a priority gets reasoned with. *Live music, and it is why you picked the*
*place* invites: would I mind? is turning it down a bigger cost? I did enjoy that place with a
band once. The answer to "would I mind" is almost always no — which is the **7.1 average out of**
**10** across sixteen sheets, the **nine that never say a clear no**, and the four factors
everybody agreed with.

An image cannot be reasoned around. It asks only: **do I want to be in that room.** The cards
become images eventually, so writing them as images now tests the real mechanism.

## The cost, stated plainly

**This reopens the leak the v8 rules were written to prevent.** Every card fault of the last week
came from scene-description carrying a second factor — *everyone reaching across the table* was
`AFFIL` hiding inside a `FOOD` card. Evocative cards leak. That is why they were banned.

The judgement is that **a leaky card that discriminates beats a clean card everybody agrees with**.
The pair-agreement alarm is what catches the leaks, so it matters more here than it did under v8.

**Known residual leaks, accepted rather than solved:**

- `LIVE` A implies a crowd — *everyone turns the same way*
- `AFFIL` A implies a populated room — *the rest of the room*
- `HERIT` B is carried by materials, which sits close to `SCEN`

A venue cannot be shown without showing whether people are in it.

## What this costs the experiment

v8.2 — the plain-language rewrite of the four weak factors — **never reached a single respondent**
before v9 replaced it. That test is abandoned, not completed. The sixteen sheets already collected
are on v8 or v8.1; everything from here is v9; nothing is on v8.2.

## Reading the results

Unchanged. Average the two cards. A gap of 50+ is a broken pair — and under v9 that alarm is the
main defence, so a card that trips it twice should be rewritten rather than interpreted.


## v9.1 — the faults in the first draft (2026-09-10)

The first v9 draft was image-shaped but still let the reader answer a question about **themselves**
instead of about the room. Five distinct faults, all caught by the user on reading:

| Fault | Where it was | Why it breaks the card |
|---|---|---|
| **Indecisiveness as a trait** | *a menu you would read twice*, *change twice before leaving the house* | Reads as "am I indecisive?" — a relatability question, and one a lot of people say yes to about themselves. Nothing to do with the factor. |
| **Universally positive** | *everyone has clearly thought about what they are wearing* | Nobody wants to be in a room where everyone is dressed badly. Same fault as v8, wearing better clothes. |
| **Scarcity read** | *not one empty table* | Reads as sold out — if every table is taken, where does the reader sit? |
| **Dismissal read** | *people standing anyway* | "Anyway" is a shrug. It makes the room sound like a compromise. |
| **Loneliness read** | *the same six faces from first drink to last* | Even someone whose friends are the whole point hears an insult. |

Two more: `HERIT` B (*stone, brass, a floor worn smooth in a path*) was too writerly to parse
quickly, and `AFFIL` A (*your table, and the rest of the room happening somewhere else*) needed
working out.

**And "room" is the wrong noun for `CROWD`.** It builds a picture of a small space crammed with
people. `CROWD` uses **place**.

## The device that replaced them: contrast inside the factor

Several cards now name the alternative outright — *packed from end to end, **not somewhere quiet
and calm***; *a proper live act **rather than a DJ***. That forces a choice where a plain
description allowed agreement.

This is **not** the banned comparative. What was banned was contrast against a *different* factor,
which imports a second dimension. Contrasting a factor against its own opposite is just making the
scale visible.

## Leaks still in, and accepted

- `CROWD` A contrasts with *quiet and calm* — calm touches `ENRG`
- `LIVE` B contrasts with *a DJ* — that is `ENRG` territory
- `POL` B says *the place styled to match* — brushes `SCEN`
- `SCEN` B says *architecture* — brushes `HERIT`

Each was taken deliberately: the contrast is what makes the card discriminate, and a card nobody
disagrees with measures nothing. The pair-agreement alarm is the check.

# v9.2 — four cards that anybody would have said yes to

`SCEN` A, `FOOD` A, `LIVE` A and `NOV` B all failed the same test: they described something
almost nobody turns down. A memorable meal, talented musicians, a good-looking room — nobody
scores those low, and a card everybody agrees with sorts nobody. Each now says *this above the
other things*, which gives the people it is not for something to actually reject.

`SCEN` A is written in the testers' own vocabulary on purpose. **Good aesthetic** is the phrase
this group already uses; a card that has to be translated first is a card that gets reasoned

## v9.3 — two merged cards, after reading the ten-card form (2026-09-10)

Both changes come from `CARD_READABILITY.md`, which was written on the back of them.

`SCEN` ended on **highlight**, the one word that makes the card discriminate, and the reader was
stopping at *good aesthetic … design and architecture*. Moving the claim off the end and making it
personal — **the main highlight of the place for you** — puts the test somewhere it gets read.

`PLAY` opened on *a table with a game*, which anchors the whole card to a board game. Someone who
would happily spend the night at an arcade or a bowling alley was answering a narrower question
than the factor asks. **or you on your feet** widens it without losing the first image.

Neither needs a rebuild. A merged wording appears only in the short branch, so `updateMainCards`
can rewrite it in place and the link survives.

## 2026-09-15 — the merged set is retired in the app

The app deck is adaptive (`ASSESSMENT_ENGINE.md` §2): every factor starts with card `A`, and card
`B` joins only if the first swipe was mild. The merged `S` cards existed to carry both angles when
only one card would ever be shown; adaptive shows `B` whenever it is needed, so `S` has no role in
the app. It stays on the test forms, whose 10-card branch still uses it.

Consequence for the images: every `A` card has to carry its factor cleanly on its own.
