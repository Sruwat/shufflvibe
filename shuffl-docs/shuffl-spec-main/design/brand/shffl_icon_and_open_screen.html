SH:FFL · the icon and the open screen

The animal is the icon; the full lockup is the first thing inside. The chameleon keeps its own tongue — the night skeleton, rising with the stops growing as the crowd does — and the name is written separately with the card mark standing in for the colon, at the optical gap of 10 we tightened to earlier. Two marks, one sentence: the animal that changes, and the night it is being built into.

The open screen — horizontal lockup

PRIMARY · PINK AND CYAN THROUGHOUT
One palette across both halves: the chameleon’s pink is the left card, its cyan night is the right card. The two marks read as one object because the colour runs straight through them — the tongue and the cards are the same two hues doing the same job at different scales.
ALTERNATIVE · CARDS BOTH PINK, CROWD CYAN
The structural fix applied to the cards only: one hue on both cards, cyan kept for the crowd. Removes the two-parties-meeting read from the card mark while keeping the chameleon exactly as locked. This is the version I would take forward — it answers the dating-app problem without splitting the palette in two.
ARCHIVO 800 INSTEAD OF BEBAS
The same lockup with the display face swapped. Archivo is wider and heavier, so the name holds more of the composition and the chameleon stops being the loudest thing. Bebas keeps the animal first, which is probably right for an open screen.

OPEN SCREEN · LIGHT
STACKED, LARGE
Stacked is the orientation an open screen actually needs — a phone is portrait and the horizontal lockup would have to shrink to about a third of the screen width to fit. On light the night goes to teal and the disc inverts, which are the two changes already recorded for the chameleon.

The icon it opens from

A wide animal in a square tile is the real tension here. Fitted, it uses about 40 per cent of the height and the tile looks half empty. Two ways out: turn it onto the diagonal, or crop to the head.

A · FITTED
The whole animal, scaled to the width. Correct and weak — all three chameleon cues are present but the thing is tiny, and at 60px it is a pink smear.
B · TURNED ONTO THE DIAGONAL
Rotated 22 degrees so the animal runs corner to corner. A square’s diagonal is 40 per cent longer than its side, so this buys real size for free and keeps the curl, the casque and the eye all in frame. This is the one I would ship.
C · CROPPED TO THE HEAD
Casque, turret eye, open mouth, tongue leaving frame. Biggest and boldest, and it holds at 32px — but it drops the tail curl, which was one of the three cues that stop this reading as a fish.

The open question

This settles the card mark’s palette by implication. If the chameleon is locked on pink and cyan and the two marks appear together on the open screen, the cards cannot be marigold or purple without the screen carrying two unrelated palettes. So the marigold, vermilion, neon and purple explorations are now either dead or they belong to a campaign layer that never sits beside the chameleon. Worth deciding which, because it is the difference between one brand and two.
