SH:FFL · neon

Neon is a treatment before it is a palette. What makes a sign read as neon is that it is a lit tube on a dark wall, with a halo around it — not that it is a bright colour. So the sheet runs two sections: the same high-chroma pairs as filled cards with a glow behind them, and then the cards redrawn as tubes. The ground is darker here than on previous sheets (#030305), because a halo needs somewhere to fall off to.

Nine neon pairs, filled cards with a halo

N1 · NEON RED + ACID LIME
The two colours real tubes actually come in. Neon proper is an orange-red; the green is a different gas. Put them together and it reads as signage rather than as a brand palette.
N2 · TANGERINE + LIME
Analogous and both hot. No pairing read at all because they sit beside each other on the wheel — the mark looks like one light source, not two.
N3 · MAGENTA + ACID LIME
Keeps the brand pink and replaces the blue with the loudest thing available. Magenta and lime is a rave palette, not a romance palette — the pink stops being soft the moment it is next to a green that aggressive.
N4 · VIOLET + LIME
UV and acid. The most club-specific pair here — this is what a blacklight room actually looks like. Violet is the risk: it is a short step from violet to the purple every streaming app uses.
N5 · ONE COLOUR · NEON GREEN
Single hue, disc dark, crowd lit. Reads as one sign rather than two cards, and green is the most legible neon at distance, which is why exit signs are green.
N6 · ONE COLOUR · NEON RED
The same structure in the true neon hue. Warmest and most nostalgic — this is a bar sign in a window, which is the right association for what the product does.
N7 · YELLOW + NEON RED
Sodium against neon — the two colours a street at night is actually lit by. Highest combined brightness on the sheet, and the hardest to look at for long.
N8 · TURQUOISE + TANGERINE
Where the blue line is. Turquoise is a green-cyan, not a blue, and paired with orange instead of pink it does not read as a dating app — but it is the closest this sheet comes.
N9 · ICE + NEON RED
A white tube is a real neon too, and the cheapest to make. One colour plus white is the most restrained neon available and the easiest to extend into a system.

The tube treatment

A neon sign is a bent tube, so everything becomes a line. The cards lose their fill and become outlines, the disc becomes a drawn ring, and the crowd stays solid because eight small circles are the one thing you cannot render as tube without it turning to mush.

T1 · TUBE · NEON RED
Nothing is filled. The cards are the glass, the letters are the glass, the disc is the glass. This is the version that actually looks like a sign rather than like a logo with a glow applied to it.
T2 · TUBE · LIME + TANGERINE
Two gases in one sign, with the crowd in white. The disc in a third colour is what a real multi-tube sign does — each colour is a separate tube, so each shape gets exactly one.
T3 · TUBE · MAGENTA + LIME
The brand pink as glass. Pink loses its softness completely once it is a line instead of a field — this may be the cheapest way to keep the colour you own and lose the coding.
T4 · TUBE · ICE, ONE COLOUR
All white glass with a single red disc. The quietest neon, and the one that would still work printed flat, because a white line on black survives losing its halo.

The catch

N3 ON LIGHT
A halo on white does nothing. Glow is light added to a dark ground; on paper there is no headroom above white, so the filter just softens the edges and the mark looks slightly out of focus. Every neon option here needs a separate flat version for light grounds — that is the real cost of this direction.
T1 ON LIGHT
The tube is worse: a lit line on a white page is just a thin line, and the whole read depends on the glow that is no longer visible. If the mark has to live on light at all, the tube version cannot be the primary.

The glow is the first casualty and the tube is the second. By 64px wide the halo has merged with the shape it surrounds and is just making the mark look blurry, and the tube outlines have closed up into solid bars. Neon is a header, poster and story treatment — the app icon has to be the flat version of whichever palette you pick, which is an argument for choosing a pair that works flat first and glows second.
