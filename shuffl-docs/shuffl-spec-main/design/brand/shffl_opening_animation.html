SH:FFL · the opening animation

The cards swipe out of the room and leave the name behind them. Both cards start square on and stacked under the disc; they fan apart and travel outward; SH comes out of the pink card and FFL out of the cyan; the room turns throughout and stops when they do. The letters are painted behind the cards, so nothing is masked — separation is what reveals them, which is why it reads as physical rather than as a fade.

Six keyframes

0 ms · STACKED
Both cards square on, one behind the other, sitting under the room. The letters are there but tiny and hidden behind the cards.
250 ms · THE SWIPE STARTS
The cards fan apart and begin to travel outward. The room is already turning and has snapped up to full size.
530 ms · LETTERS EMERGE
SH comes out of the pink card and FFL out of the cyan — they are painted behind the cards, so separation alone reveals them.
810 ms · TRAILS OPEN
The ghost cards lag behind the solid ones, which is what makes it read as a swipe rather than a slide.
1120 ms · SETTLING
Cards near their rest angles, letters near full size. The room is finishing its turn.
1400 ms · AT REST
The wordmark exactly as locked — optical gap 10 either side, cards at ±26°, ghosts at ±13°.

The timing, for the developer

Total 1400 ms. Every track is ease-out cubic (1-(1-x)³) — fast away from the centre, slow into rest, which is how a swiped card actually behaves. Nothing bounces: an overshoot would make the cards look light, and these are meant to have weight.

The ghost finishes 140 ms after the card it trails. That lag is the whole trick — identical timing makes the pair read as one thick card, and the trail only becomes a trail when it is late.

Two things to hold the developer to. First, the letters are behind the cards in paint order — no masks, no clipping paths; if they are drawn on top the reveal stops working and no amount of timing fixes it. Second, the room’s rotation ends last, 140 ms after everything else has settled, so the mark arrives and then quietly stops rather than everything landing on one beat.
