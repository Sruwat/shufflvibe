SH:FFL · two shades of purple

A value pair fixes the structural half of the problem for free. Two shades of one hue cannot read as two parties meeting — there is only one colour present, so the cards look like one deck splitting rather than two people arriving. That was the thing the pink-and-cyan arrangement was doing regardless of which hues sat in it.

The risk is a different one. Purple on black with a glow is close to the territory streaming and crypto already occupy, and the blue end of purple walks back toward the read you were trying to leave. The pairs below are ordered from the magenta side to the blue side so you can see where that starts; the last one is flagged.

Six purple pairs, flat

P1 · MAGENTA-PURPLE + VIOLET
The warm end. One card leans toward magenta, the other toward true violet — far enough apart to be two shades, close enough to stay one colour. Nearest to the pink you already own, so it is the smallest move from where the brand is now.
P2 · NEON PURPLE + AUBERGINE
The widest value gap here. One card is nearly lit and the other nearly black, which gives the mark real depth — but the dark card needs the white disc beside it or it disappears into the ground.
P3 · ORCHID + GRAPE
A mid-value pair, neither extreme. The most usable of the six — both cards read clearly on either ground, and neither one is doing anything loud enough to date badly.
P4 · LILAC + ELECTRIC VIOLET
Light against electric, with the crowd in lilac so the disc joins the family. The softest option, which is worth naming as a risk rather than a feature given what you are steering away from.
P5 · VIOLET + DEEP PLUM
Electric over deep plum. Plum is the only one of these darks that still reads as a colour rather than as black, so the mark keeps two hues without gaining two parties.
P6 · IRIS + LILAC · FLAGGED
This is where purple becomes blue. Iris sits on the blue side, and paired with a pale lilac the mark starts reading soft and romantic again. Included as the boundary, not as an option.

The same pairs lit

Purple gains more from a halo than any other hue, because a violet glow on black is the one piece of colour the eye reads as genuinely light-emitting rather than painted. It is also exactly why the territory is crowded.

P1 LIT
The warm pair lit. The magenta card throws a pink halo and the violet throws a blue one, so the glow quietly reintroduces the two-colour read the flat version had removed.
P3 LIT
Orchid and grape lit. The halos are close enough in hue that they merge into one field instead of two — which is the behaviour you want, and it only happens with a tight pair.
P5 LIT
Electric and plum. Only the bright card glows, because a dark colour has nothing to emit — so the mark lights asymmetrically, which looks like one sign with one tube lit.

As tube

T · ORCHID + GRAPE
The darker purple struggles as glass. A tube is a thin line, and a thin line in a deep colour on a dark ground has almost no contrast to work with — grape nearly vanishes while orchid sings.
T · TWO LIGHT PURPLES
Tube needs both shades at the bright end. Orchid against neon purple keeps the value gap small and the brightness high, which is the only way a two-shade tube reads as two shades.

On the light ground

P1 ON LIGHT
Both hold. Purple is one of the few colours that keeps its identity against paper-white without darkening — the opposite of cyan, and the reason this direction is cheaper to maintain than the original.
P3 ON LIGHT
Orchid is the one to watch. It is pale enough that on white it starts to lose its edge, though the dark letters hold it together. Grape is untouched.
P5 ON LIGHT
The best of the three on light. Deep plum against paper is a genuinely strong contrast, and the electric card keeps every bit of its chroma.

Small — the whole mark at 150px, 96px and 64px wide

P1 · MAGENTA + VIOLET
P3 · ORCHID + GRAPE
P5 · VIOLET + PLUM

Two shades of one hue cost you something at small sizes. A pair that is far apart in hue stays two cards all the way down; a pair that differs only in value starts to look like one card and its shadow. P5 survives furthest because the gap between electric violet and deep plum is wide enough to read at 64px, which is the argument for choosing a wide value gap over a subtle one.
