SH:FFL · people, not holes

The size variation was the mistake. Irregularly-sized round holes clustered on a surface is the pattern that triggers trypophobia, and I introduced it by varying the radii. Everything below uses one size and varies only placement and lean, and four of the five stop being circles altogether. Overlap now does the job size variation was doing — and overlapping forms cannot read as holes, because a hole does not cover another hole. Type is Archivo 800 white throughout.

Five crowds — one size, placement only

A · FIVE, TWO ROWS
Head and shoulders, all the same size, two rows with the front row lower. Overlap is doing the work that size variation used to — and overlap cannot trigger the pattern, because the forms are joined rather than isolated.
B · SEVEN, THREE ROWS
Denser. Reads as a room rather than a group, and the back row being partly hidden is what makes it feel like there are more than seven.
C · HEADS ONLY, EVEN SIZE
Circles again, but every one the same radius — which is the specific thing that was wrong before. Simplest to render, and still the most hole-like of the four.
D · CAPSULES
Featureless uprights. Abstract enough to be anything, and the lean makes them people — a row of straight capsules would read as a barcode.
E · HEAVIER FIGURES
Same arrangement, broader shoulders and a bigger head. Built for the icon — thin figures close up at 32px, these do not.

3 and 6 make the circle the loudest thing in the mark, which fights the cards — the crowd is meant to be the still point. 1 is the safest and 5 is the most distinctive: a dark face with pink figures keeps the circle quiet while still carrying the brand colour, and it is the only one of the six that needs no white at all.
