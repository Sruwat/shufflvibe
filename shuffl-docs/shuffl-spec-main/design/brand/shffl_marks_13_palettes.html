SH:FFL · getting the dating app out of it

The hues are only half of why it reads that way. Pink and blue is the specific coding, but the other half is structural: two different colours meeting around a shared circle is a picture of two parties pairing up, whatever the hues are. So there are two independent levers here, and the first section pulls each one on its own before the palettes start.

Two one-change fixes, before changing the palette at all

WHERE IT IS NOW
Two hues, one each side, symmetric. Pink and blue is the dating-app signal, but the arrangement is doing as much work as the colours — two parties approaching a shared middle.
FIX 1 · ONE COLOUR, BOTH CARDS
Same pink, both cards. Nothing has met anything; it is one deck splitting, which is what the product actually does. This alone removes most of the dating read without touching the brand colour.
FIX 2 · NEIGHBOURS, NOT OPPOSITES
Pink and saffron — two colours next to each other on the wheel instead of across it. Reads as one thing shifting hue rather than two things meeting. Still warm, still pink-based.

Twelve palettes, on the 01 geometry

All with the eight and the open centre. Where both cards share a hue the disc goes dark and the crowd takes the colour, so there is still something for the colour to do in the middle.

01 · MARIGOLD + VERMILION
Warm analogous — marigold into vermilion. Neighbours on the wheel, so it reads as heat rather than as a pair. Marigold is also the most Delhi colour there is, and nobody has ever built a dating app on it.
02 · ONE COLOUR · MARIGOLD
The same hue on both cards with the crowd carrying it too. Three statements of one colour — the most ownable version here, and the easiest to recognise at a distance.
03 · ACID LIME + INK
Club flyer, not dating profile. Acid lime on black is the loudest thing on this sheet and the furthest from soft. But look at the right card: a black card disappears on a black ground, so this one needs an outline on dark or a lighter neutral — see 10.
04 · LIME + DEEP GREEN
Same lime, paired down into a deep green. A value pair rather than a hue pair — light and dark of the same family, which never reads as two parties.
05 · BRASS + OXBLOOD
The upmarket end. Brass and oxblood is a bar interior, not an app — leather, low light, a good list. Costs you the neon energy the rest of the product has.
06 · SAFFRON + PLUM
Warm against deep. Plum is the one dark here that is not black, which keeps the mark from going flat while staying well away from blue.
07 · ELECTRIC YELLOW · ONE COLOUR
Maximum visibility, zero romance. Yellow is a warning colour and a taxi colour and a sodium-lamp colour. It is also the hardest of these to use on a light ground.
08 · JADE + MARIGOLD
Green and gold. Opposites, so the pairing read comes back a little, but green-and-gold codes as festive rather than romantic — it is the one complementary pair here that survives.
09 · CLAY + TEAL
A softer, earthier version of the original idea. Teal is as close to blue as this sheet goes — included so you can see where the line actually is.
10 · VERMILION + CREAM
One hot card, one neutral. The cream reads as paper, so the mark looks like something printed rather than something rendered.
11 · ONE COLOUR · VERMILION
Single-hue again, in the hottest red that is not a danger signal. Closest in energy to the current pink while carrying none of its coding.
12 · PINK + LIME
The smallest possible change: keep the pink, replace the blue. Lime is aggressive enough that the pair stops reading as soft, and you keep the brand colour you already own.

Four of them on the light ground

01 ON LIGHT
Both survive the flip untouched. Warm colours hold their value against paper-white in a way cyan never did — this is the practical argument for a warm palette, not an aesthetic one.
02 ON LIGHT
Single-hue marigold with a dark disc. Nothing is redrawn for either ground, which was the property that made 03 the safe card mark two sheets ago.
03 ON LIGHT
Acid lime is the one that struggles. It is a light colour, so on a light ground the card loses its edge and the mark starts to feel like it is missing a piece.
11 ON LIGHT
Vermilion holds as cleanly on light as pink does, and reads hotter at the same saturation because it sits closer to the middle of human colour sensitivity.

Note on the chameleon: 02 is locked — pink body, cyan night. If the card mark moves off pink and cyan, the two marks stop sharing a palette, which is either a problem or a deliberate split between the product mark and the campaign mark. Worth deciding rather than discovering.
