SH:FFL · the crowd counted, and the colour

Every circle inside any one disc is the same radius — that is fixed and stays fixed. The radius does fall as the count rises (6.0 at seven down to 4.4 at twelve), because more circles in the same disc either get smaller or get closer, and holding the gap constant near 4 units is what keeps it reading as people rather than as perforation.

Seven counts, evenly distributed

WHERE WE GOT TO · 6
Last round — even size, loose placement.
A · SEVEN · HEX
One in the middle, six around it. The only arrangement here that is perfectly even — every circle sits exactly the same distance from every neighbour. No other count can.
B · EIGHT · OPEN CENTRE
Eight on the edge, nothing in the middle. This is the app's own room screen — members ringing the blend. The hole in the centre is the price.
C · NINE
Eight plus a centre. The middle one sits further from the ring than the ring sits from itself — a small unevenness, but visible once you look for it.
D · TEN
Nine plus a centre. The densest version that still reads as separate people rather than as texture.
E · TEN · ROWS 3/4/3
The same ten in rows instead of rings. Evenly spaced, but it fills a hexagon inside a circle, so the corners of the disc stay empty.
F · TWELVE · TWO RINGS
Three inside nine. Twelve is where it stops being people and starts being pattern — here to mark the ceiling, not to use.

At 32px the gaps close before the circles do. Seven still reads as seven; by ten the ring has fused into a dotted band, and twelve is a ring of noise. Seven is the highest count that survives the smallest size you actually ship — the favicon and the notification badge.

Ten colour combinations — cards, letters, disc, crowd

All on the even seven. Each one moves four things at once, because they are not independent: a dark disc only works while the cards stay bright, and coloured letters only work once the cards go quiet. Cells on white are the combinations that need a light ground — black cards are invisible on black.

01 · THE ONE WE HAVE
Pink and cyan cards, white type, white disc, ink crowd. Four hues and three values — the busiest version, and the one everything else gets measured against.
02 · DARK DISC, PINK CROWD
The disc stops competing. It goes near-black and the crowd carries the pink, so the cards stay the brightest things in the mark — which is correct, because the cards are what move.
03 · DARK DISC, WHITE CROWD
Same idea, white crowd. Quieter than 02 and the most legible of all ten at small sizes, because white on near-black is the highest-contrast pair in the palette.
04 · ONE COLOUR, TWO CARDS
Both cards pink, cyan only inside the disc. Prints in two inks, and makes pink unambiguously the brand colour rather than half of a pair.
05 · INK CARDS, COLOURED TYPE
Cards go black, the colour moves into the letters. The most restrained version, and the only one where SH and FFL are themselves the pink and the cyan. Shown on light because black cards need a light ground.
06 · PINK DISC
Inverted: black cards, pink disc. The room becomes the loudest thing, which reads as a venue mark rather than an app mark — the wrong story for what this is.
07 · CROSS-COLOURED TYPE
Each card carries the other card's colour in its letters. Ties the two halves together, but cyan on pink is the weakest contrast pair available and it shows.
08 · PINK AND WHITE
One pink card, one white. Drops cyan entirely — worth seeing, because cyan is the colour that collapses on a light ground and this version never has to care.
09 · GOLD CROWD
Gold is artwork-only in the system — it means the promoted night and nothing else. Using it here spends that meaning on the logo. Here so the answer is a decision, not an oversight.
10 · MONO, FOR ONE INK
No brand colour at all. Every logo needs this version — embroidery, a stamp, a newspaper, a sponsor wall, anywhere someone else controls the printing.

Four of them on the light ground

01 ON LIGHT
Cyan all but disappears on paper-white — the known failure of the palette, and the reason the light ground gets checked separately every time. The disc needs a dark edge here or it dissolves into the page.
03 ON LIGHT
A dark disc needs no edge on light, so this one survives the flip untouched. 03 is the only combination that is identical on both grounds.
04 ON LIGHT
Two pink cards invert cleanly, and the cyan sits inside the disc where its weakness on light never gets tested.
10 ON LIGHT
The single-ink version, inverted. This has to work before any of the others count as finished — it is the one that ends up on a rubber stamp.
