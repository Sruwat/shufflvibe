SH:FFL · the swipe, rebuilt

The first attempt failed for one reason and it was the same reason in four of the six: a card swiped outward leaves its trail behind it, and behind it is exactly where the circle sits, so every trail was drawn and then covered up. The circle is smaller here and the cards are further out, which opens the gap the motion needs. The circle still never moves. Trails are still less tilted than the card casting them, because a flung card straightens as it goes.

A · GHOST TRAIL
Two fading copies, each straighter than the one in front. Now that they are not behind the circle, this is the clearest of the six — and still the busiest.
B · SPEED LINES
Three strokes behind each card, sitting in the open gap. Keeps its weight when everything shrinks, because a stroke does not fade the way an opacity does.
C · MOTION SMEAR
A gradient dragged behind each card, clipped so it cannot spill above them — which is what produced the dark wedge last time. Softest, and the first to break in one-colour printing.
D · THE DECK BEHIND
A stack still waiting and two already gone — the only one that says there are more cards. It needs the crowd moved underneath to make room, which costs the symmetry. Rebuilt: last time the stack read as a grey smudge.
E · THE ARC
Three dots per side, shrinking along the path the card took. Last time these were specks hidden by the circle; at this size they read, but they are still the first thing to vanish when the mark is small.
F · ONE GHOST, CLIPPED
One trail each, and the cards pushed until they break the frame. One ghost says motion as well as two, and the clipping does the rest. Still the one I would take — fewest parts, and nothing in it depends on opacity being legible.
F AS THE LOCKUP
The mark still occupies the colon's slot, so the wordmark and the icon remain one object. The ghosts read as a shimmer at this size, which is the right amount of them in a line of type.
AT ICON SIZE
The crowd dots go and the ghosts stay, which is the right way round: the movement is the idea and the dots were detail. On the pink tile the whole mark is one colour and still reads as two cards leaving.
