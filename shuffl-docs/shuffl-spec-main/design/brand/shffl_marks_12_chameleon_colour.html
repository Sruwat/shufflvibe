SH:FFL · the chameleon, night as the tongue

Same drawing throughout — the curl, the casque and the turret eye are the three cues that say chameleon, and the tongue is the night skeleton: it rises left to right and the stop circles grow with the crowd, which is literally how the drawing works in the app. Only the colour changes below, which is the one thing a chameleon ought to be flexible about.

01 · GREEN · WHERE WE STARTED
The literal reading. Green says chameleon faster than any other colour, and that is its whole case — it is also a colour the brand does not own, so the mark would be importing a hue the rest of the system never uses.
02 · PINK BODY, CYAN NIGHT
Brand-correct and unmistakable. The animal is the brand colour and the night it catches is the cyan. Loses the green cue, so it reads as a lizard first and a chameleon on second look.
03 · CYAN BODY, PINK NIGHT
The reverse. The night is now the brand colour, which is the right emphasis — the product is the night, not the animal. But cyan is the colour that collapses on light grounds, and here it is the biggest shape in the mark.
04 · PINK TO CYAN
The animal is itself the colour change. Pink at the tail, cyan at the head, and the night goes white so it stays separate from both. This is the only version where the gradient is doing conceptual work rather than decoration — the chameleon that is mid-shift.
05 · CYAN TO PINK
The same gradient reversed, so the head is pink and the night leaves a pink mouth as a white line. Weaker than 04, because the brightest part of the animal now sits next to the brightest part of the night and they compete.
06 · INK BODY, PINK NIGHT
Black animal, pink night, and the eye is the only other colour in the mark. The most restrained of the eight, and the one that would survive being printed by someone else. Shown on light, because a black animal needs a light ground.
07 · WHITE BODY, PINK NIGHT
The inverse of 06. Pairs with 10a — same logic, neutral body with the colour carried by the thing that moves. On a light ground this one has to flip to 06.
08 · OUTLINE, PINK AND CYAN
One weight throughout, so it strokes in a single pass — a stamp, a neon tube, an embroidery file. The tail curl is the thinnest thing here and also the part doing the most work, so it is the first thing to fail when this gets small.

Three of them on the light ground

02 ON LIGHT
Pink holds exactly as it does on dark. The night has to darken — cyan at full strength is invisible here, so it drops to a deeper teal and stops matching the app.
06 ON LIGHT
Unchanged. A black animal with a pink night needs nothing redrawn for either ground, which is the same property that makes 03 the safest of the card marks.
04 ON LIGHT
The gradient survives, but the cyan end fades out against the page and the animal looks like it is dissolving at the head rather than changing colour.
