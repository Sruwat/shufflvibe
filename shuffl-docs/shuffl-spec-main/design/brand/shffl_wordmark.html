SH:FFL · the wordmark

The mark is not beside the name, it is the punctuation inside it. SH, the card mark where the colon goes, FFL — at the optical gap of 10 either side. The crowd is the eight with the open centre, one radius throughout. The thing that makes the spelling odd is the thing that makes it the logo.

PRIMARY · BOTH CARDS PINK, CROWD CYAN
One hue on both cards, cyan kept for the crowd, and a white rim on the disc so it holds its edge against a dark ground. One deck splitting, not two things meeting — which is what takes the pairing read out of it.
ALTERNATIVE · PINK AND CYAN CARDS
The two-colour version, with the white disc and ink crowd. Louder, and it matches the chameleon’s pink and cyan exactly — at the cost of bringing back the two-parties read the primary removes.

On the light ground

PRIMARY · LIGHT
Letters to near-black, disc keeps its dark face and white crowd, rim drops away. Nothing else changes.
ALTERNATIVE · LIGHT
Cyan collapses toward black on paper-white, so the right card stops being cyan and starts being dark. The known failure of that pair.
ARCHIVO 800 INSTEAD OF BEBAS
Wider and heavier. Archivo makes the name the loudest thing, which is right when the wordmark stands alone and wrong when it sits under the chameleon.

Down in size

The crowd is the first thing to go. At 360px wide you can still count eight; by 230px it is a ring; by 150px it is a dark dot between two cards. The cards and the letters survive all the way down, which is what matters — the name still reads as SH:FFL even when the room inside it has closed up.
