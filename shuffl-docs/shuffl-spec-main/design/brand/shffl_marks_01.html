SH:FFL · mark explorations

Two directions, four treatments each, plus the wordmark. The colon is doing work before any mark does — every stop in the product is written 21:00, 23:30, 01:00, so the name already carries the app’s own clock separator. Both ideas below can sit in that slot. Each is shown on the dark ground and one is shown on light, because a mark that only works on black is half a mark.

IDEA 1 · TWO CARDS, ONE CROWD

1A · LITERAL
The two swipe directions and the crowd circle, as described. Reads immediately and is the busiest of the four at small sizes.
1B · OUTLINE CARDS
Cards recede, the crowd is the subject. Holds at 24px, which 1A does not.
1C · MID-SWIPE
Cards pushed to the edge and clipped, so the mark implies motion rather than depicting two objects. The ring is the crowd growing.
1A ON LIGHT
Two colours only. The cyan card becomes near-black, because cyan on paper-white loses its edge.
THE LOCKUP — THE CROWD IS THE COLON
This is the one worth arguing for. The mark is not beside the name, it is the colon — so the wordmark and the icon are the same object, and the thing that makes the spelling odd becomes the thing that makes it memorable.
AT ICON SIZE
Three dots inside the circle vanish below ~30px. Solid circle survives, so the small version drops the crowd dots and keeps the silhouette.

IDEA 2 · THE CHAMELEON, TONGUE AS THE NIGHT

2A · THE WHOLE ANIMAL
The tongue is the skeleton — it rises left to right and the stop circles grow with the crowd, which is literally how the drawing works. The chameleon changes colour daily and so does the user: it is the only animal that is already the product. Needs width, so it is a header mark rather than an app icon.
2B · SOLID, NO TONGUE
One colour, no tongue, mouth still open. The curl, the casque and the eye are what say chameleon — drop the curl and it is a fish, which the first attempt at this was.
2C · OUTLINE
Single weight throughout, so it can be stroked in one colour on anything. The thinnest thing here is the tail curl, which is also the part doing the most work.
2D · THE TONGUE WRITES THE NAME
The tongue leaves the mouth, lands as the first stop, and the colon is the next two stops on the same line. The animal and the name share one baseline instead of sitting next to each other.
2A ON LIGHT, AND SMALL
The tongue is the first thing to die at small sizes — by 60px it is a pink smudge. The icon keeps the head and the eye only.

THE WORDMARK ON ITS OWN

PLAIN
The colon in the live colour, nothing else. This is the floor — any mark has to beat it.
TWO CARDS AS THE COLON
The colon is the two swipe cards, squared off. Idea 1 reduced to its smallest possible form.
THE COLON GROWS
Two stops, the second fuller than the first — the crowd circle swelling, which is what a press-and-hold does in the skeleton game. A night in two dots.
ON LIGHT
Works unchanged. The colon stays pink on both grounds, which is what makes it the brand asset rather than the letters.
